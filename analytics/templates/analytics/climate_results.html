Data Sources & Parameters
  • NASA POWER Data: Satellite-derived meteorological parameters at 0.5° resolution
  • Time Period: Typically 20+ years of historical data for robust climatology
  • Parameters: ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN, T2M, T2M_MAX, T2M_MIN, PRECTOTCORR, RH2M, WS10M
  • Tip: All data represents agrometeorological conditions suitable for crop modeling
  • Note: Data quality validated for agricultural applications, may differ from ground station measurements
Agricultural Parameters
  • Reference Evapotranspiration (ET₀): Potential water use by well-watered grass reference crop
  • Crop Water Requirement (CWR): Actual water need for specific crops (mm/day)
  • Irrigation Need: Difference between CWR and natural precipitation
  • Tip: CWR varies by crop type, growth stage, and local conditions
  • Note: Default CWR calculations assume standard crop coefficients - adjust for specific crops
Technical Calculations
  • Moving Averages: 30-day centered moving average for trend visualization
  • Climatology: Long-term monthly averages based on complete historical record
  • Trend Analysis: Linear regression for decade-scale changes in parameters
  • Tip: Climatology helps identify typical seasonal patterns for crop planning
  • Note: All calculations use agricultural standard methodologies (FAO, ASABE)
Interpretation Guidelines
  • Solar Radiation: Values >5 kW-hr/m²/day indicate excellent solar potential
  • Temperature: Most crops thrive between 10-30°C average temperatures
  • Precipitation: Compare with CWR to assess irrigation requirements
  • Water Balance: Positive values = irrigation needed, negative = water surplus
  • Tip: Use multiple charts together for comprehensive agricultural planning
  • Note: Local soil conditions and crop varieties will affect actual water requirements
Agricultural Planning Tips
  • Crop Selection: Match crop water requirements to local precipitation patterns
  • Planting Dates: Use temperature charts to avoid frost risks and optimize growing seasons
  • Irrigation Scheduling: Plan based on monthly water deficit patterns
  • Solar Integration: High radiation months correlate with peak irrigation energy needs
  • Tip: Coordinate crop water needs with seasonal solar availability for optimal solar irrigation
  • Note: Consider crop-specific sensitivity to temperature extremes and water stress
Climate Change Considerations
  • Temperature Trends: Warming trends may extend growing seasons but increase water demand
  • Precipitation Patterns: Changing rainfall distribution affects water availability
  • Extreme Events: Increased frequency of droughts/floods requires resilient planning
  • Tip: Use trend analysis to anticipate future conditions and plan adaptation strategies
  • Note: Decade-scale trends provide early warning of climate shifts affecting agriculture
